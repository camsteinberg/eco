An apple is a round, edible fruit produced by an apple tree (Malus spp., among them the domestic or orchard apple; Malus domestica). Apple trees are cultivated worldwide and are the most widely grown species in the genus Malus. The tree originated in Central Asia, where its wild ancestor, Malus sieversii, is still found. Apples have been grown for thousands of years in Eurasia before they were introduced to North America by European colonists.


== Description ==
The apple is a deciduous tree, generally standing 2 to 4.5 metres tall in cultivation and up to 9 metres in the wild. When cultivated, the size, shape and branch density are determined by rootstock selection and trimming method. The leaves are alternately arranged dark green-coloured simple ovals.

Blossoms are produced in spring simultaneously with the budding of the leaves and are produced on spurs and some long shoots. The flowers are white with a pink tinge that gradually fades.


== Nutrition ==
A raw apple is 86% water and 14% carbohydrates, with negligible content of fat and protein. == Cultivation ==
Many apples grow readily from seeds, but they do not breed true. Instead, cultivars are propagated by grafting onto rootstocks, which control the size of the resulting tree. There are more than 7,500 cultivars of apples, resulting in a range of desired characteristics for cooking, eating raw, and cider production.

Apple trees are self-incompatible and must cross-pollinate to develop fruit. During the flowering each season, apple growers often provide pollinators to carry the pollen between the trees.


== Storage ==
Commercially, apples can be stored for some months in controlled atmosphere chambers to delay ethylene-induced ripening. Apples are commonly stored in chambers with higher concentrations of carbon dioxide and high air filtration.
