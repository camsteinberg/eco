The skeleton is the structural frame that supports an animal. There are two general types of skeleton: the exoskeleton, which is the stable outer shell of an organism, and the endoskeleton, which forms the support structure inside the organism. The human skeleton is an internal framework that in adulthood consists of 206 bones.

== Structure ==
The human skeleton is composed of 206 bones in adulthood, down from around 270 at birth, because several bones fuse together during growth. The largest bone is the femur, and the smallest is the stapes in the middle ear.

The skeleton is subdivided into the axial skeleton and the appendicular skeleton. The axial skeleton comprises the skull, the vertebral column, the ribs and the sternum, and accounts for 80 of the bones in an adult.


== Function ==
The skeleton serves six major functions: support, movement, protection, production of blood cells, storage of minerals, and endocrine regulation. Bone marrow inside the larger bones produces red and white blood cells.

Calcium and phosphorus are stored in bone tissue and released into the bloodstream when circulating levels fall. This makes the skeleton an active organ rather than an inert scaffold.


== Development ==
Ossification begins in the embryo and continues into the early twenties. Cartilage models are gradually replaced by mineralised tissue in a process called endochondral ossification.
